How does it work?

Type, choose and copy; the grammar is the tool's job.

Type the number as it is

Western or Arabic-Indic digits, with or without thousands separators. The input is cleaned automatically, and the “Number used” appears next to the text so you can check it.

Choose the currency or unit

Arab, world and crypto currencies with their subunits, plus weights, lengths, areas and times, or a plain number with no unit.

Grammar done right

Singular and plural, hyphenated tens and the subunit joined with “and” in English; gender agreement, the dual and the counted noun's case in Arabic, as used on cheques and in accounts.

Copy and use

Copy the text with one click, and add “… Only” if you like. For long lists, convert them all at once and export them to CSV.

Frequently asked questions

Which languages can it write amounts in?

English, Arabic and French. Pick the “Output language” at the top of the converter, and the amount is written in that language with the name of the currency and its subunit. The ready-made text changes to suit the language, such as “… Only” in English. The “Spelling of hundred” option applies to Arabic only, so it is hidden for the other languages; if a unit has no wording in a language, a message says so.

How are amounts written in English?

In Title Case, as on cheques: “One Thousand Two Hundred Fifty Saudi Riyals and Seventy-Five Halalas”. Tens are hyphenated (Twenty-One), there is no “and” inside the number (Two Hundred One), and the scales are Thousand, Million, Billion and Trillion. One takes the singular (One US Dollar); every other amount, including zero, takes the plural.

Unit names use British spelling, as in Gulf and Levant banking: Metre, Litre, Tonne. Every choice is documented in the project specification on GitHub.

How are fractions written: cents, halalas and fils?

Every currency has a subunit and a ratio: the US dollar has 100 cents, the Saudi riyal 100 halalas, and the Kuwaiti, Bahraini, Jordanian and Iraqi dinars 1,000 fils. The subunit is joined to the currency with “and”: “One Thousand Five Hundred Kuwaiti Dinars and One Hundred Twenty-Five Fils”. Units without parts, such as a piece or a carton, reject a fraction with a clear message instead of silently dropping a real quantity, and a plain number reads its decimals after “Point”.

What if I type more decimal places than needed?

The amount is rounded to the nearest subunit, and a half is rounded up: 10.125 Saudi riyals is written “Ten Saudi Riyals and Thirteen Halalas”. The calculation works on the text of the number, not on floating-point decimals, so a value computed in a spreadsheet such as 0.30000000000000004 US dollars is written “Thirty Cents”. To reject the number instead of rounding it, choose “Show an error” in the formatting options.

Does it handle Arabic grammar properly?

Yes. Each currency and unit has a grammatical gender, and so does its subunit, so Arabic numbers agree with the counted noun, the dual and the plural are used where they belong, and the noun takes the case its number requires, following the usage of finance and accounting departments. For Arabic you can also choose how “hundred” is spelled: مائة, the usual spelling on cheques, or the modern مئة.

Are the numbers I type sent to a server?

No. The whole conversion runs in your browser: no external fonts, no trackers, no ads. The only request that leaves the site asks GitHub for the number of the latest Windows release, so the download button can point at it, at most once an hour, and it carries no number you typed. After the first visit the tool is stored in your browser, so it works even offline. Details are in the privacy policy.

Is there a Windows app?

Yes. InstaWords for Windows 10 and 11 is free, works offline, needs no admin rights, has an English or Arabic interface and prints cheques and vouchers. Download the installer directly v2.0.0, or see the release notes and the portable version.

Can I convert amounts inside Excel?

Yes. Download the free VBA module from the Excel page and import it once into your workbook, then type =TAFQIT(A1, "USD", , , , , "en") in any cell. The function runs on your computer without internet, and it is tested automatically against the same reference cases as this page.
